Definition

What is a mutation?

A change in sequence relative to a reference. Three things must be pinned down before the word means anything at all.

1 · Relative to what?

Reference genome? The patient's own baseline? Population consensus? "Mutation" is always a comparison — name the thing you compared to.

2 · In what fraction?

A virus is a population of genomes. A change in 100% of them and in 2% of them are both real — and mean completely different things.

3 · With what confidence?

Sequencers make errors. A 0.3% signal may be a mutation, or may be noise. This question will bite us later.

Our file offers two comparisons: ref vs strain → consensus mutations (Sanger sees these). strain vs base counts → minority variants (deep sequencing only).

Question 1 · relative to what

14% of this genome differs from the reference. Almost none of it is "mutation."

This sample is almost certainly a non-subtype-B virus measured against a subtype-B reference (HXB2 / NC_001802.1).

Those 1,287 differences are subtype differences — evolution that happened decades before this infection existed. They did not "arise" in this patient.

A difference from the reference is not a mutation that arose in your sample.

The reference is a historical accident — one man's virus, sequenced in 1983. It is not a wild-type ideal, and it is not "normal."

This single confusion produces more bad clinical genomics than any statistical error.

Why reading frame decides everything

An indel sized in multiples of 3 adds or removes whole codons — the protein is shorter or longer, but still readable.

Any other size shifts the reading frame: every downstream codon is garbled and the protein is usually destroyed. Same "one mutation" — wildly different consequence.

Types · axis 2 · your lie detector

Transitions vs transversions

Transition (Ti) — purine↔purine A↔G, or pyrimidine↔pyrimidine C↔T. Chemically similar bases; easy swaps.

Transversion (Tv) — purine↔pyrimidine. A bigger chemical change.

There are 8 possible transversions but only 4 transitions. So pure chance predicts Ti/Tv = 0.5.

Real data runs far higher — transitions are mechanistically favoured (deamination of methyl-C → T) and more often synonymous, so selection removes them less often.

Diversity

Divergence and diversity are different axes

Divergence asks: how far from the reference?

Diversity asks: how variable is the population within itself?

A sample can sit far from the reference yet be perfectly uniform, or sit right on it and be a seething swarm. This is the quasispecies concept: an infection is not a virus — it is a cloud of related genomes.

Shannon entropy

H = −Σ pb ln pb

0 = invariant · 1.386 = all four bases equally likely

Nucleotide diversity π

π̂ = [n/(n−1)] · (1 − Σ pb²)

The probability that two randomly drawn reads differ at a site.

The n/(n−1) correction matters. Without it, low-coverage sites look artificially less diverse — purely because you sampled fewer reads. That is a sampling artefact masquerading as biology.

Diversity · by gene

Mind the overlapping reading frames

HIV-1 is a masterclass in genomic compression: genes overlap, in different reading frames. vpu sits inside env; tat/rev exon 2 sit inside env.

A naive case_when() assigns each site to one gene and silently discards every overlap — 685 sites here. Use a long-format join so a site can belong to several genes.

env sits high — the surface protein, under relentless antibody pressure, and tolerant of change. pol — the drug target, structurally constrained — sits lower. Diversity maps onto selection.

Minority variants

Everything Sanger cannot see

Sanger sequencing sees ~20% and above. Anything rarer is invisible — buried in the chromatogram noise. Deep sequencing, at thousands of reads per site, can in principle reach 1% or lower.

Variants in that gap — present, real, sub-consensus — are minority variants. In HIV they matter enormously:

  • Minority drug-resistance mutations at 1–20% are associated with increased risk of virological failure — especially NNRTIs, which have a low genetic barrier.
  • They are archived from past treatment, or transmitted — and invisible to standard genotyping.
  • Under drug pressure, a 1% variant can sweep to fixation in weeks.
The result · take a good look

451 minority variants. Every one significant at FDR < 1%.

We did it properly. If the per-base error rate is ε, then under the null "this is just error" the alt count is Binomial(n, ε). So we tested every site against ε = 0.2%, and corrected for multiple testing with Benjamini–Hochberg.

The reversal

Ti/Tv collapses at low frequency

This is damning. At 0.1% the ratio is 0.09 — not merely random (0.5), but far below it. Random error would give 0.5. Something worse than random is happening: a systematic, direction-specific artefact.

At our 1% threshold, Ti/Tv sits at exactly 0.50. Roughly half of those 451 "significant" variants are noise.

Diagnosis

Read the spectrum, name the culprit

Look at what dominates low VAF: A>C, T>G, G>T, C>A — all transversions.

And notice they form reverse-complement pairs (A>C ≡ T>G; G>T ≡ C>A). That strand symmetry is the fingerprint of a chemical/technical artefact — not biology.

  • G>T / C>A — the classic 8-oxoguanine (OxoG) oxidative damage signature, introduced during library prep.
  • A>C / T>G — a well-documented context-specific sequencing artefact.

Above 2% the spectrum flips to G>A, A>G, C>T — transitions. Real biology.

Why the statistics didn't save us

The test wasn't wrong. Its assumption was.

At ~4,000× depth, a 1% variant means ~40 supporting reads. Against a null of ε = 0.2%, forty reads is overwhelmingly significant — p is astronomically small.

So the test passes every single call. It never had a chance to reject anything.

The binomial test assumes error is uniform at 0.2% everywhere. Real error is context- and site-specific — at some sites it runs at 2%. The model cannot see that, so it confidently certifies artefacts.

Statistical significance is not biological reality.

A p-value only ever tests the model you handed it. With enough depth you can get vanishingly small p-values for pure garbage.

Deep sequencing does not protect you from this — it guarantees it. Depth makes everything significant.

The fix

Throw most of it away

Filter out the artefact signature (A>C, T>G, G>T, C>A) and raise the threshold to 2% — justified empirically, from Ti/Tv and the spectrum, not by convention.

Ti/Tv jumps from 0.50 to 5.25. We discarded 83% of our variants, and what remains finally looks like biology.

Independent validation

The virus signs its own work

If our surviving calls are real, they should carry biological signal. HIV has a famous one.

Host APOBEC3G deaminates cytosine during reverse transcription, producing G>A hypermutation. The virus's vif gene exists precisely to destroy APOBEC3G — that is its whole job.

G>A climbs from 1.6% to 20% as VAF rises. Sequencing error has no reason whatsoever to do that. A specific, known, host-driven biological process does.

This is independent confirmation that the surviving calls are real — and that the low-VAF tier is not.

Burden testing

Trade resolution for power

Single-variant tests work for common variants. For rare ones they fail — and the reason is purely statistical. If a variant appears in 3 people out of 5,000, nothing on Earth has power to associate it with anything.

The burden test's move is collapsing: stop testing variants, start testing units — a gene, a region, a domain.

Instead of "does variant X associate with disease?", ask "do carriers of any rare variant in gene G differ from non-carriers?"

You no longer know which variant matters — but you can now detect that the gene matters at all.

The plain burden test's fatal flaw: it assumes every variant in the unit pushes the same way. If half are harmful and half protective, they cancel — and it sees nothing. That is why SKAT exists.

Burden · an honest note

We have one sample. So let's be precise about what we can claim.

What we cannot do

A classical burden test compares cases vs controls across many individuals. We have one sample. We cannot run one — and you should be suspicious of anyone who claims otherwise.

What we can do

The same machinery, in its within-sample form: collapse variants into genes, test each against a background rate. This is exactly how regional mutation-enrichment scans work in cancer genomics — and it drills the identical logic.

THE NULL

Minority variants fall uniformly across the genome, at the genome-wide rate. Under that null, the count in a gene is Binomial(gene length, background rate). Test it. Correct it. Report the q-value.

Nine genes = nine tests. At α = 0.05 you expect ~0.45 false positives by chance. Always report the q-value.

Burden · the payoff

The same test, before and after

vif — a false positive

Its "enrichment" was driven by artefact-signature calls — 77% of vif's 1% calls were A>C/T>G/G>T/C>A. A confident, FDR-corrected, entirely false result.

env — real signal

fold 1.33 → 1.74 · q 0.0007 → 0.011

It got stronger when the noise was removed — the hallmark of real signal, and exactly what HIV biology predicts for the antibody-facing envelope protein.

The lesson

FDR controls chance. It is blind to bias.

Our q-value was 0.0005 and the result was still garbage.

Multiple-testing correction did not save us. It was never designed to. FDR controls for chance — it has nothing at all to say about systematic bias.

No amount of statistics rescues contaminated input. Only the biology told us the truth:

  • Ti/Tv sitting on the 0.5 noise line
  • The OxoG strand-symmetric spectrum
  • The APOBEC3G G>A gradient

Every one of those checks is a domain check, not a statistical one. They work because we know what HIV does — and what a sequencer does when it is wrong.

Know your data's failure modes, or your p-values will lie to you with perfect confidence.

Takeaways

Six things to leave with

1 · "Mutation" is a comparison

State: relative to what, at what frequency, with what confidence. 14% of this genome differs from HXB2 because it is a different subtype — not because the patient mutated.

2 · Frequency ≠ rate

One timepoint gives a snapshot, never a speed.

3 · Diversity ≠ divergence

Independent axes — and diversity maps onto selection (env high, pol constrained).

4 · Threshold + depth define your count

451 variants at 1%; 75 at 2% with filtering. Neither is "the truth." Report both numbers, or report nothing.

5 · Ti/Tv is your lie detector

0.5 = noise. If your minority calls sit at 0.5, they are noise — no matter how small the p-value.

6 · Statistics cannot rescue contaminated data

We got q = 0.0005 for vif and it was pure artefact. Only the biology told us the truth.
